# Film Evidence: Nepal Has Hydropower. Why Isn't It Enough?

*[Nepal Has Hydropower. Why Isn't It Enough?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_35zVh1yD1U)* is a 16-minute TransparentGov film published on 2 October 2026. It asks why one electricity system exported about 552 GWh to India in Bhadra and imported about 357 GWh in Falgun of the same fiscal year, 2024/25. This page lists every factual claim the film makes, where the wiki covers it, and the primary source shown on screen.

## The argument in brief

1. **The reversal is seasonal, not a one-off.** In Bhadra, domestic generation of about 1,848 GWh exceeded demand of about 1,293 GWh. In Falgun, generation fell to 605 GWh against demand of about 1,013 GWh. See [[seasonal-mismatch]] and [[claim-timing-not-volume]] (C-001).
2. **Headline potential is the wrong measure.** Megawatts, gigawatt-hours, dependable megawatts and duration answer different questions. See [[firm-power]] and [[claim-mw-not-equal-value]] (C-006).
3. **Daily pondage is not seasonal storage.** Kali Gandaki A shifts about six hours a day; only the 106 MW Kulekhani cascade holds water across seasons. See [[claim-pror-not-storage]] (C-009) and [[kulekhani-cascade]].
4. **The fleet followed its incentives.** Energy-priced contracts and stand-alone project packages favoured run-of-river and peaking run-of-river plants. See [[claim-ror-dominance]] (C-005) and [[ppa-pricing]].
5. **Reservoirs need many obligations to hold for years.** Tanahu shows the delivery burden. See [[tanahu-hydropower]].
6. **Start from the dry-season order, not from a technology.** Five jobs fill it: reduce, produce, shift, deliver and protect. See [[storage-deficit]], [[solar-hydro-complementarity]] and [[claim-transmission-immediate-blocker]] (C-019).
7. **Contracts decide what is built.** The 2082 reservoir directive separates declared available capacity from contract energy. See [[erc-reservoir-ppa-directive-2082]].

## Corrections

> [!warning] At 1:12 the narration says WECS "could not obtain the original 1966 study". WECS 2019 attributes the 83,500 MW figure to Shrestha (1966), but that statement about the original study has not been found in the WECS report. The 83,500 MW figure is still a theoretical estimate; only the remark about the 1966 study is unsupported.

## What the pictures do and do not show

- Satellite views are dated Copernicus Sentinel-2 images. The Kulekhani sequence (25 Nov 2024, 5 Mar 2025, 24 Apr 2025) shows visible water extent, not volume or level.
- Plant dots use DoED licence-area midpoints, not facility locations. The fleet-type map shows only the 44 plants (2,577 MW) whose hydraulic type is published in the sources checked. The other 169 plants (about 1,333 MW) are not typed.
- Cross-border links and trade arrows are schematic, except the named [[dhalkebar-muzaffarpur]] 400 kV line. The 552 and 357 GWh figures are national monthly totals, not flows on that line.
- The 24-hour load diagrams, the valley illustration and the Tanahu works illustration are schematic; their bars carry no data except where stated.

## What the film does not claim

- It does not say run-of-river plants were a mistake. They added energy and helped end load-shedding.
- It does not name a single cause of the fleet mix. It describes incentives that made some paths easier to repeat.
- It does not give a national dependable-capacity number or a dry-season deficit forecast. The "Falgun order" is a planning method, not a figure.
- It does not show Tanahu as a national solution, or any combination of the five jobs as already contracted.
