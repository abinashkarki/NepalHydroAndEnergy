# Storage and System Flexibility

## Three different time problems

- **Seasonal flexibility** moves monsoon water or energy into dry months.
- **Daily flexibility** moves daytime energy into the evening peak.
- **Operating flexibility** manages ramps, outages, forecast error and congestion over minutes and hours.

These are related but not interchangeable. A battery sized for an evening peak is not a seasonal reservoir, and an import contract is not domestic storage.

## What exists in the public baseline

The Kulekhani cascade totals **106 MW**—60 MW at Kulekhani I, 32 MW at Kulekhani II and 14 MW at Kulekhani III—and generated **279.8 GWh** in FY2024/25. Only Kulekhani I provides the reservoir; II and III reuse its regulated downstream releases. The cascade's MW and annual GWh do not disclose usable storage volume, dispatch duration or current seasonal state. [[kulekhani-cascade]] and [[storage-deficit]] preserve those distinctions.

Other flexibility sources include peaking run-of-river operation, cross-border imports, dispatchable reservoir projects, demand response and future battery storage. The presence of a technology in a plan or tender does not verify an operating resource.

## Evidence trail

| Evidence | What it establishes | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| [[nea-annual-report-fy2024-25]] | Kulekhani cascade capacity and FY2024/25 annual generation; imports in the annual system balance | No public reserve, state-of-charge, dispatch-duration or plant-level congestion series |
| [[wb-water-sector-diagnostic]] | A national water-sector storage requirement of 29.86 km³, about 85 MCM of active dam storage, and JICA planning estimates of 1,993–3,154 MW of storage hydropower by 2032 | Water-sector requirement is not an electricity-only target; JICA figures are study outputs, not projects under construction |
| [[national-water-plan-nepal]] | Historical runoff and transboundary water-planning context | A 2005 planning baseline, not current storage-operation evidence |

Capacity, energy, stored volume and response speed are separate quantities. This wiki does not convert one into another without a source-backed duration, operating rule or state-of-charge series.

## Solar changes the daily problem

Solar can reduce dry-season daytime scarcity but increases the importance of evening shifting, demand timing and distribution readiness. Planning must identify which problem a proposed resource solves, for how long and with what evidence.

## Evidence gaps

- A national operating BESS registry.
- Reserve requirements and reserve performance.
- Ancillary-service procurement and settlement.
- Public hourly load and renewable forecasts.
- Plant-level dispatch, congestion and curtailment.
- Reservoir or battery state of charge.

Until those exist, this wiki does not infer a national flexibility margin from installed MW.
