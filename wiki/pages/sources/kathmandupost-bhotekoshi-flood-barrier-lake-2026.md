# Kathmandu Post — New barrier lake in Nepal raises fresh flood threat, China warns (2026)

## Summary

This 27 August 2026 report records a secondary hazard created by the 26 August flood: a landslide-dam barrier lake near the Chhochen Khola–Purepu Tsangpo confluence, close to the Nepal–Tibet border where the combined river becomes the Bhote Koshi — the principal upper tributary of the Trishuli.

## Key Claims

- China's Ministry of Water Resources estimated lake volume at about **2 million cubic metres** on 27 August, already overflowing, with another **3 million cubic metres** expected within three days and a high breach risk (CCTV via Global Times).
- The lake formed near the confluence of the Chhochen Khola and Purepu Tsangpo; the Chotchen and Purupe rivers originate in Tibet and merge before crossing into Nepal, where the river is known as the Bhote Koshi.
- The main flood struck the Bhote Koshi in Rasuwa on Wednesday morning (26 August), sweeping through **Timure** and **Syabrubesi** before flowing downstream toward the Trishuli.
- Chinese authorities state they are monitoring the lake and running flood simulations to support rescue and flood-control operations.

## Relevance

The barrier lake makes the 2026 event a compound, ongoing hazard rather than a single flood: a breach would re-inundate the same corridor whose hydropower works were damaged on 26 August. The Purepu system is the same glacier area that produced the 8 July 2025 supraglacial-lake flood documented in [[ndrrma-rasuwa-glacial-flood-sitrep-2025]], which is central to the repeat-hazard argument on [[glof-risk]] and [[bhotekoshi-trishuli-flood-2026]].

## Limitations

- Volume figures are Chinese-authority estimates relayed through state media; Nepal-side confirmation and satellite verification were not available at publication.
- The report does not give coordinates for the barrier lake; the map layer places it approximately near the stated confluence.
- Breach risk is a forecast, not a deterministic outcome; the lake could drain gradually or persist.
