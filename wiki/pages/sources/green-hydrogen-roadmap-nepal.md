# Green Hydrogen Roadmap for Nepal

## Summary

Strategic roadmap for producing green hydrogen from Nepal's surplus monsoon hydropower via electrolysis. Envisions an entirely new domestic industrial ecosystem absorbing stranded wet-season power.

## Key Data Points

- Primary target: domestic green **ammonia/fertilizer** production (Nepal imports 100% of chemical fertilizers)
- A domestic green fertilizer plant could absorb up to **500 MW** of continuous baseload power
- Replacing coal in Nepal's cement clinker production would require **227,743 tonnes** of green hydrogen annually, needing **2,002 MW** of dedicated hydropower
- Projected viable production cost: **$2.15/kg** by 2035
- Government adopted Green Hydrogen Policy in 2024 with tax exemptions and regulatory framework
- Commercial pilots targeted for 2025–2027, industrial scale by 2035

## Relevance to Project

Central to [[green-hydrogen-nepal]] and the demand-creation argument in [[domestic-led-hydro-strategy]]. Addresses [[stranded-generation]] by creating domestic demand for surplus monsoon power.

## Limitations

- **Roadmap, not committed project pipeline.** The ammonia, fertilizer, and cement-substitution figures are planning scenarios rather than financed projects.
- **Demand-creation assumptions.** The roadmap assumes industrial offtake, electrolysis deployment, water availability, and fertilizer-market integration that are not yet demonstrated in Nepal.
- **Cost trajectory uncertainty.** The projected $2.15/kg production cost by 2035 depends on equipment-cost declines, power-price assumptions, and utilization rates.
- **Policy still early-stage.** The 2024 Green Hydrogen Policy creates a framework, but pilot delivery, permitting, and industrial procurement models remain unsettled.
