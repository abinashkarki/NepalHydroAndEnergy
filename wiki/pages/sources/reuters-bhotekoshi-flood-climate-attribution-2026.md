# Reuters — Climate change boosted risk of devastating Himalayan flood, experts say (2026)

## Summary

This 27 August 2026 Reuters wire (republished by The Kathmandu Post) is the first expert-attribution reporting on the 26 August flood. It describes the initiating mechanism, the transboundary damage chain, and the climate context — while explicitly withholding causal certainty.

## Key Claims

- The disaster probably began with a **massive ice-rock avalanche on the northern slopes of Langtang Lirung** into the **Lhende Khola** river on the Tibetan side; exact cause was still being analysed at publication.
- At least six major hydropower plants and infrastructure at the **Gyirong Port** trade hub were damaged in a multi-stage hazard chain across borders; river channels were altered more than **100 km downstream**, near the Indian border.
- Expert sources (Earth Sciences New Zealand's Simon Cox, ICIMOD's Farooq Azam, City University of Hong Kong's Benjamin Horton) frame the event as consistent with climate-driven destabilisation of high-mountain rock and ice — warming reduces ice support on steep slopes, boosts meltwater, and changes freeze-thaw patterns — but caution against naming climate change the sole immediate trigger; Azam suggests heavy snow melt coupled with seismic activity as probable triggers.
- The piece situates the event in a sequence: the 2025 flood at Gyirong on the Tibet–Nepal border, the 2024 Thame glacial-lake outburst, and the 2023 Sikkim outburst — "similar catastrophic surges … on a smaller scale."
- A UN 2023 finding is cited: Nepal lost close to one-third of its ice cover in just over 30 years, with glacier melt 65 percent faster in the preceding decade.

## Limitations

- The mechanism is expert inference, not a completed investigation; the initiating process could be revised by later field and satellite studies.
- Attribution statements are about risk conditions, not event-level causation; the article itself says so and the wiki should not sharpen the claim.
- The six-damaged-plants figure for the Tibetan side is attributed reporting, not a verified inventory.
