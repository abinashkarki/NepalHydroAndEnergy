# IPO Hydropower Bailout

## Summary

How Initial Public Offerings (IPOs) and rights share issuances can shift hydropower-project financing risk onto retail investors — and what the public record can, and cannot, demonstrate about that pattern in Nepal.

**This page is under research.** The current evidence establishes a set of official offering records, not a verified sector-wide "bailout" inventory. SEBON's scans and Nepali-language PDFs make extraction laborious, but they are a research task rather than a basis for treating the evidence as unavailable.

## Simple Explanation

The concept is a testable financing-risk question: when a hydropower company raises public equity before stable earnings, or later seeks rights capital, are investors funding productive expansion, balance-sheet repair, or both? The answer must be established issuer by issuer from prospectuses, financial statements and post-offer disclosures.

## What SEBON's records establish

SEBON has official records for a partial cohort of hydropower IPOs and rights-share filings. Issuer financial statements now provide post-offer evidence for three of the four rights issues; the table distinguishes prospectus plans from later reported outcomes.

| Issuer | Official record located | What the records establish | Remaining limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ankhu Khola Jalvidhyut | IPO, approved prospectus and audited FY 2081/82 statements | NPR 1.20 billion rights cash plus NPR 134.642 million auction premium; net borrowing repayment NPR 482.416 million; total borrowings fell by NPR 498.474 million | The audited statements do not trace the full planned NPR 800 million Ankhu Khola-2 equity allocation; other-equity investments rose by NPR 135.225 million |
| Joshi Hydropower | IPO and approved right-issue prospectus | 1:0.65; NPR 241.41 million planned: NPR 155.71 million bank principal/interest, NPR 56.835195 million promoter advance, NPR 28.864805 million repairs | Final allotment, actual application and subsequent audited balances remain unreconciled |
| People's Power | Approved prospectus, closure notice and issuer Q3 FY 2082/83 statement | Paid-up capital rose by the full NPR 316.3 million offer amount; reported long-term borrowing fell from NPR 318.024 million to zero | The post-offer statement is unaudited and has no separate proceeds-application schedule |
| Terhathum Power | Approved prospectus and issuer Q3 FY 2082/83 statement | Issuer says the 1:1 issue completed and proceeds repaid long-term bank debt; paid-up capital doubled to NPR 800 million and long-term borrowings fell by NPR 540.032 million | The statement is unaudited; the debt reduction exceeds the offer amount and cannot all be attributed to rights cash |
| Rasuwagadhi | Local-offering record | A SEBON prospectus record exists | A comparable IPO amount, a rights issue, and a use-of-proceeds finding |

The earlier five-row inventory has been retired. It referred to six companies while listing five, did not provide row-level primary documents, and treated pipeline values as completed-rights-issue amounts in several cases. [[sebon-hydropower-offering-records]] preserves the official regulatory trail now available in the wiki. Ankhu Khola is now the first issuer in this cohort with a primary prospectus-level proceeds allocation: one-third of the planned NPR 1.20 billion was earmarked for partial bank-loan repayment and two-thirds for investment in a second project. That is evidence of a mixed expansion-and-balance-sheet transaction, not proof that the whole issue was a debt bailout.

Claims about NEPSE suspensions, insider trading, promoter sales or debt-repayment outcomes are not retained here until an official NEPSE notice, issuer filing or audited statement is linked.

## The Chilime contrast

Chilime Hydropower's 2010 IPO occurred after **seven years of profitable operations** and distributed a 35% dividend within a year. This is a documented contrast case, but whether newer issuers were pre-revenue, distressed or using equity for debt retirement must be established from each issuer's own record. See [[chilime]] for the pre-IPO profitability context.

## What is missing

The approved-prospectus extraction is complete for Ankhu Khola, Joshi
Hydropower, People's Power and Terhathum Power. Across this bounded
four-issuer cohort, each prospectus assigns some rights proceeds to bank
or other financing liabilities, but the allocations differ: Ankhu Khola
combines expansion equity with partial bank repayment; Joshi combines
bank debt, a promoter advance and repairs; People's Power and Terhathum
direct the stated proceeds to bank debt. This establishes planned uses,
not actual cash application or a representative sector-wide rate.

Post-offer reconciliation is now substantially complete for Ankhu Khola,
People's Power and Terhathum Power. The records show that public equity
did fund balance-sheet repair in all three, but they do not support one
uniform transaction type or a sector-wide rate. Ankhu Khola mixed debt
reduction with a stated project-investment purpose; People's Power and
Terhathum show post-offer debt reductions consistent with their stated
bank-repayment purposes. Only Ankhu Khola's outcome is audited.

The remaining bounded gaps are:
- What was Joshi Hydropower's final allotment and actual split between bank debt, promoter advance and repairs?
- Where is Ankhu Khola's full planned NPR 800 million Ankhu Khola-2 equity allocation reflected after the offer?
- Can audited FY 2082/83 statements confirm the People's Power and Terhathum quarterly outcomes?
- What risk disclosures about hydrological underperformance, PPA rate risk, and AD penalty exposure appear in hydro IPO prospectuses?
- What is the full list of NEPSE-listed hydropower companies whose rights issues completed within three years of IPO?
- What is NRB's specialized hydro loan book — the true extent of off-balance-sheet restructuring and informal forbearance?

**Next evidence steps:** obtain Joshi's allotment and post-offer financial
statement; trace Ankhu Khola-2 investment through the issuer's investment
notes; capture audited FY 2082/83 statements for People's Power and
Terhathum; then expand beyond this selected cohort using a declared
sampling rule. Only after that audit can the page quantify a
debt-retirement or "bailout" pattern.

## Common Misunderstandings

- **An IPO is not automatically a growth investment.** In distressed hydro cases, proceeds or later rights shares may function as balance-sheet repair.
- **The Chilime example is not automatically replicable.** Chilime listed after profitable operations; many newer projects list before stable earnings.
- **A SEBON pipeline entry is not a completed issue.** Review-stage terms cannot be treated as cash raised or debt repaid.
