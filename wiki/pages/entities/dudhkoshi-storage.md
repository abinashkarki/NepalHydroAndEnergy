## Summary

A proposed **670 MW storage reservoir** scheme straddling Okhaldhunga,
Khotang and Solukhumbu in the eastern [[koshi-basin]]. The February 2026
AIIB and DKJVCL materials describe a 600 MW underground main powerhouse
and a 70 MW dam-toe ecological-flow powerhouse. The project is in
pre-construction development and market sounding: AIIB financing is
proposed, not approved or effective, and no current primary source
establishes a firm construction start or commercial-operation date.

### Engineering
Dudhkoshi's current published design pairs a 220 m dam with an
underground main powerhouse (600 MW) and a dam-toe ecological-flow
powerhouse (70 MW). The current AIIB project summary and 2026
market-sounding teaser do **not** establish the previously listed 200 MW
pumped-storage component, so that figure has been removed from the
structured specification record pending a primary design source. The
project's safeguard work explicitly includes sediment, slope, seismic,
biodiversity and [[glof-risk]] assessment.

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Dam height | 220 m current basis |
| Main powerhouse | 600 MW (underground) |
| Dam-toe powerhouse | 70 MW (ecological flow) |
| Underground powerhouse | Yes |
| Type | Storage |

## Significance

Dudhkoshi matters on three documented axes:

1. **Scale.** 670 MW of proposed reservoir generation in a system where the
   entire current storage fleet is 106 MW ([[kulekhani-cascade]])
   and the only storage actually under construction is 140 MW
   ([[tanahu-hydropower]]). Dudhkoshi alone would roughly match the
   combined 2025 hydropower fleet of Nepal on energy volume and
   materially change the dry-season [[firm-power]] portfolio if built.

2. **Seasonal storage.** The reservoir and 1,252 GWh dry-season design
   figure make the project relevant to [[seasonal-mismatch]] without
   requiring the unsupported pumped-storage claim.

3. **Safeguard-intensive design.** The project is being scoped with
   landscape stabilization and sediment management built in — a
   response to Nepal's [[sediment-as-design-constraint]] reality and
   to the fact that the upper Dudh Koshi routinely receives GLOF
   pulses from the Everest region (see [[glof-risk]]).

Compare Dudhkoshi (670 MW, storage, proposed multilateral financing) to [[arun-3]]
(900 MW, peaking RoR, SJVN, export-first). Both are in the Koshi
basin, but they occupy different delivery stages and have different
operating purposes.

## Limitations & Controversies

**Four-decade gestation.** The original feasibility was ADB-supported
in the 1980s. Land acquisition restarted only in 2021. A project
that has been "about to be built" for 40 years carries a credibility
tax that any new timeline must earn against.

**Displacement footprint.** AIIB's preliminary summary reports about
3,300 affected households and approximately 290 households expected to
be physically and economically displaced. The transmission line and
access-road impacts were still being assessed at publication, so these
figures are not a final project-wide resettlement total.

**GLOF exposure.** The 2024 Thame Valley GLOF traveled down the Dudh
Koshi mainstem. A 220 m dam on a GLOF-exposed river demands a design
flood that correctly accounts for cascading lake outburst scenarios,
not a stationary climatology. How that design flood is set — and how
conservative the spillway margin is — is the safety-critical question
for the entire project.

**Financing status.** AIIB sovereign financing is proposed, not
approved, effective or disbursed. The disclosed USD 200 million amount
also covers only part of the estimated project cost.
