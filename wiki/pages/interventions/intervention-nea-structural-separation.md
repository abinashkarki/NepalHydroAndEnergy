# Decision Dossier — Power-System Role Separation and Open Access

> [!important]
> **TransparentGov assessment, not settled fact:** Better disclosure and operational rules are immediately testable. A separate system operator or penalty authority remains a policy option requiring legal, institutional and cost analysis; this dossier does not treat it as a proven cure.

## Decision Question

Which changes to dispatch governance, open-access implementation and penalty administration would improve neutrality and accountability without weakening system reliability or creating an institution that Nepal cannot yet operate effectively?

## Established Facts

- [[nea-annual-report-fy2024-25]] records electricity supplied by NEA and its subsidiaries, independent producers and imports, while NEA operates central parts of the transmission and distribution system. The reviewed PPA analysis also identifies NEA as the off-taker under standard hydropower contracting.
- Public reporting indexed at [[erc-open-access-directive-2082]] states that ERC issued the Open Access Directive 2082 in January 2026. The official directive text and implementing manuals are not archived in the current corpus, and issuance does not establish operational use.
- [[nea-standard-ppa-clauses]] and [[ad-penalty-clause-research]] describe availability-declaration and curtailment provisions through secondary legal analysis. They are not a complete corpus of signed PPAs or a formal legal opinion.
- The reviewed public corpus does not contain project-level dispatch instructions, accepted-energy records, reason codes, penalty ledgers or adjudication outcomes sufficient to measure discriminatory dispatch or sector-wide penalty exposure.
- The frequently repeated 16 percent penalty figure has no identified basis in the reviewed standard-PPA analysis and must not be used as a sector aggregate.

## Unknowns

- The official text, current implementing rules and observed transactions under Open Access Directive 2082.
- Whether project-level dispatch differs systematically by ownership after controlling for location, grid conditions, contract terms and plant availability.
- The content and variation of representative signed PPAs, including curtailment and dispute provisions.
- Penalties assessed, waived, disputed and recovered by project and year.
- The statutory amendments, staffing, systems and recurring cost required for a separate operator or penalty authority.
- Whether a ring-fenced unit inside NEA could deliver adequate independence, and how its performance would be audited.

## Comparable Cases

No sourced comparable institutional-reform case is included in the current corpus. International system-operator models vary in legal mandate, market design, utility ownership, metering and settlement capability.

**Transfer limitation:** the existence of an independent operator elsewhere does not show that separation would produce neutral dispatch in Nepal, nor that Nepal currently has the data, staffing and legal framework needed to operate that model.

The Barahi material is not used as a proven comparison: the current source record does not include a primary audited statement or adjudication record sufficient to establish the claimed penalty outcome.

## Response Options and Trade-offs

| Option | What it would involve | Potential advantage | Material limitation or risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Publish operational evidence | Release dispatch instructions, accepted energy, reason codes, constraints and penalty outcomes with contract-sensitive redactions | Makes the problem measurable and supports later institutional design | Disclosure requires data standards, audit controls and legitimate confidentiality rules |
| Operationalise open access within current institutions | Publish the official directive, eligibility, wheeling charges, metering, scheduling, settlement and dispute procedures | Tests third-party access before a larger institutional redesign | Formal rules may not alter practice without enforcement, capable metering and settlement systems |
| Ring-fence dispatch inside NEA | Separate staff, accounts and decision criteria; publish performance and conflict-of-interest controls | Potentially faster than creating a new statutory body | Formal separation inside one organisation may not create practical independence |
| Create an independent system operator | Transfer dispatch functions to a legally separate, governed institution | Creates a clearer institutional separation if mandate and capability are credible | Requires legislation, transition design, funding, technical capacity and accountability safeguards |
| Separate penalty calculation or appeals | Give ERC or another neutral body a defined review or adjudication role | Could strengthen procedural fairness | Jurisdiction, evidence standards, appeal rights and administrative capacity remain unresolved |

## TransparentGov Assessment

**Preferred evidence-led sequence:** publish the official open-access instrument and its implementation rules, establish auditable dispatch and penalty records, and test the current framework before choosing a permanent institutional model. Ring-fencing and independent operation should be compared through a published legal and capability assessment.

This assessment would change if primary dispatch and penalty evidence demonstrates a systematic conflict that cannot be controlled inside the current structure, or if official open-access implementation records show that the present model works in practice.

## Monitoring Triggers

- An official archived copy of Open Access Directive 2082 and its implementing manuals.
- A completed third-party transaction using the open-access framework.
- Publication of project-level dispatch, constraint and penalty records.
- A bill, regulation or government decision on system-operator or penalty-administration roles.
- A primary PPA corpus or authoritative legal interpretation.
