Summary

Nepal's first and — at the time of writing — only operational 400 kV cross-border link. Every exported MWh, including the Nepal–Bangladesh Electricity Trade Route allocation, transits this line. It is the linchpin of Nepal's current export strategy.

Key Facts

Parameter Value
Voltage 400 kV (operated initially at 220 kV, upgraded 2018)
Status Operational
Commissioned February 20, 2016
Nepal terminal Dhalkebar substation (Dhanusha)
India terminal Muzaffarpur (Bihar)
Length ~140 km (≈40 km Nepal + ≈100 km India)
Developers Cross Border Power Transmission Company (CBPTC) — NEA + Indian JV
Financing ADB, KfW
Current transfer ~800–1,200 MW monsoon, ~300–500 MW dry season
Interconnection ID dhalkebar_muzaffarpur

Significance

  • Export artery. Until Gorakhpur–New Butwal 400 kV Cross-Border Interconnection and Inaruwa–Purnea 400 kV Cross-Border Interconnection commission, this is the only 400 kV door out. Roughly 95% of Nepal's 2024–25 export revenue moved through it.
  • IEX access. Unlocks the Indian Energy Exchange market — the day-ahead pricing that has made monsoon exports profitable for the first time in Nepal's history.
  • Bangladesh corridor. Also the Nepal-side leg of Nepal–Bangladesh Electricity Trade Route, which then transits through India on the Baharampur–Bheramara link.

Limitations & Controversies

  • Single point of failure. No 400 kV backup cross-border exists yet. An outage collapses export revenue immediately.
  • Indian-side evacuation. Muzaffarpur terminus's onward capacity into India's Eastern Regional Grid is approaching saturation under peak Nepal export scenarios.
  • Dispatch veto. Scheduling is set by India's NRLDC — Nepal cannot unilaterally increase flow even when physical capacity exists.
