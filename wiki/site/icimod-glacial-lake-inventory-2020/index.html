Summary

This ICIMOD–UNDP research report (DOI 10.53055/ICIMOD.773) maps glacial lakes in the Koshi, Gandaki and Karnali basins, using Landsat imagery of 2015, and identifies 47 potentially dangerous glacial lakes (PDGLs). Of these, 21 are in Nepal, 25 are in the Tibet Autonomous Region of China and drain into Nepal, and one is in India.

Key Findings

  • 3,624 glacial lakes were mapped. 1,410 of them are at least 0.02 km². After screening, 47 PDGLs remained: 42 in the Koshi basin, 3 in the Gandaki and 2 in the Karnali (Table 5.5).
  • The 47 PDGLs are ranked I (31 lakes), II (12) or III (4). Rank I lakes have the highest breach concern (Table 5.6).
  • The 21 Nepal PDGLs fall in these sub-basins: Tamor 4, Arun 4, Dudh Koshi 9, Tama Koshi 1, Trishuli 1, Marsyangdi 1 and Humla/Mugu 1.
  • Tsho Rolpa and Imja Tsho stay in Rank I even though their water levels were lowered earlier.
  • Each lake ID encodes the lake's longitude and latitude to 0.001° (for example, GL086925E27898N is Imja Tsho).

Limitations

  • The source does not tabulate elevation, area or downstream hydropower exposure for individual PDGLs. Those fields are blank in glof_dangerous_lakes.csv.
  • Ranks reflect conditions in the 2015 imagery and a remote-sensing screening, not field investigation of every lake.
  • Coordinates come from the lake IDs. They mark the lake, not an outlet or a dam crest.
