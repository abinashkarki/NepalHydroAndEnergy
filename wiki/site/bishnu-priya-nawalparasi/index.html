Summary

Bishnu Priya is a roughly 1 MWp operating IPP solar plant in Nawalparasi. The page tracks it as an early Phase I proof-of-concept for Nepal's grid-tied commercial IPP solar programme.

Why it matters

Bishnu Priya is the proof-of-concept scale — <1 MWp, nominally demonstration but commissioned on commercial terms via a PPA with NEA. It pre-dated the larger Phase I plants (Butwal Solar (Ridi Hydropower Development, 8.5 MWp), Mithila Solar PV Electric Project (Eco Power, 10 MWp)) and was the first grid-tied IPP PV plant Nepal's interconnection and PPA machinery processed end-to-end.

Its specific contribution to the learning curve:

  • Validated the 11 kV interconnection template for sub-MW IPP solar.
  • First PPA processed through NEA for IPP solar — the document formed part of the baseline for later rounds.
  • Demonstrated the Central Terai GHI numbers at operational scale, confirming satellite estimates from Global Solar Atlas (Nepal coverage).

Bishnu Priya's <1 MWp scale limits its current significance operationally — it contributes <0.1% of the solar fleet — but its place in the institutional timeline is the reason it appears in the NEA FY 2024/25 annual report inventory tables.

Limitations & Controversies

The page is strongest on programme-timeline context and NEA inventory presence. Detailed plant-level performance, exact COD documentation, land records, PPA terms, and operational availability are not independently built out here.
