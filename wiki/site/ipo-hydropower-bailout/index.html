IPO Hydropower Bailout

How Initial Public Offerings (IPOs) and rights share issuances can shift hydropower-project financing risk onto retail investors — and what the public record can, and cannot, demonstrate about that pattern in Nepal.

Summary

How Initial Public Offerings (IPOs) and rights share issuances can shift hydropower-project financing risk onto retail investors — and what the public record can, and cannot, demonstrate about that pattern in Nepal.

This page is under research. The current evidence establishes a set of official offering records, not a verified sector-wide "bailout" inventory. SEBON's scans and Nepali-language PDFs make extraction laborious, but they are a research task rather than a basis for treating the evidence as unavailable.

Simple Explanation

The concept is a testable financing-risk question: when a hydropower company raises public equity before stable earnings, or later seeks rights capital, are investors funding productive expansion, balance-sheet repair, or both? The answer must be established issuer by issuer from prospectuses, financial statements and post-offer disclosures.

What SEBON's records establish

SEBON has official records for a partial cohort of hydropower IPOs and rights-share filings. Issuer financial statements now provide post-offer evidence for three of the four rights issues; the table distinguishes prospectus plans from later reported outcomes.

The earlier five-row inventory has been retired. It referred to six companies while listing five, did not provide row-level primary documents, and treated pipeline values as completed-rights-issue amounts in several cases. SEBON Hydropower Offering Records (Selected Issuers, 2018–2025) preserves the official regulatory trail now available in the wiki. Ankhu Khola is now the first issuer in this cohort with a primary prospectus-level proceeds allocation: one-third of the planned NPR 1.20 billion was earmarked for partial bank-loan repayment and two-thirds for investment in a second project. That is evidence of a mixed expansion-and-balance-sheet transaction, not proof that the whole issue was a debt bailout.

Claims about NEPSE suspensions, insider trading, promoter sales or debt-repayment outcomes are not retained here until an official NEPSE notice, issuer filing or audited statement is linked.

The Chilime contrast

Chilime Hydropower's 2010 IPO occurred after seven years of profitable operations and distributed a 35% dividend within a year. This is a documented contrast case, but whether newer issuers were pre-revenue, distressed or using equity for debt retirement must be established from each issuer's own record. See Chilime for the pre-IPO profitability context.

The triple-authority blindspot

The interaction between offering disclosures, PPA tariff assumptions and Availability-Declaration Penalties is a research question, not a current finding. The prospectuses in the first SEBON pass have not yet been extracted and coded for hydrology, curtailment, tariff or AD-penalty risk disclosure. See NEA Triple-Authority Conflict for the institutional context.

What is missing

The approved-prospectus extraction is complete for Ankhu Khola, Joshi Hydropower, People's Power and Terhathum Power. Across this bounded four-issuer cohort, each prospectus assigns some rights proceeds to bank or other financing liabilities, but the allocations differ: Ankhu Khola combines expansion equity with partial bank repayment; Joshi combines bank debt, a promoter advance and repairs; People's Power and Terhathum direct the stated proceeds to bank debt. This establishes planned uses, not actual cash application or a representative sector-wide rate.

Post-offer reconciliation is now substantially complete for Ankhu Khola, People's Power and Terhathum Power. The records show that public equity did fund balance-sheet repair in all three, but they do not support one uniform transaction type or a sector-wide rate. Ankhu Khola mixed debt reduction with a stated project-investment purpose; People's Power and Terhathum show post-offer debt reductions consistent with their stated bank-repayment purposes. Only Ankhu Khola's outcome is audited.

The remaining bounded gaps are:

  • What was Joshi Hydropower's final allotment and actual split between bank debt, promoter advance and repairs?
  • Where is Ankhu Khola's full planned NPR 800 million Ankhu Khola-2 equity allocation reflected after the offer?
  • Can audited FY 2082/83 statements confirm the People's Power and Terhathum quarterly outcomes?
  • What risk disclosures about hydrological underperformance, PPA rate risk, and AD penalty exposure appear in hydro IPO prospectuses?
  • What is the full list of NEPSE-listed hydropower companies whose rights issues completed within three years of IPO?
  • What is NRB's specialized hydro loan book — the true extent of off-balance-sheet restructuring and informal forbearance?

Next evidence steps: obtain Joshi's allotment and post-offer financial statement; trace Ankhu Khola-2 investment through the issuer's investment notes; capture audited FY 2082/83 statements for People's Power and Terhathum; then expand beyond this selected cohort using a declared sampling rule. Only after that audit can the page quantify a debt-retirement or "bailout" pattern.

Common Misunderstandings

  • An IPO is not automatically a growth investment. In distressed hydro cases, proceeds or later rights shares may function as balance-sheet repair.
  • The Chilime example is not automatically replicable. Chilime listed after profitable operations; many newer projects list before stable earnings.
  • A SEBON pipeline entry is not a completed issue. Review-stage terms cannot be treated as cash raised or debt repaid.
