Summary

This data page benchmarks Nepal's hydropower system against mountain and hydro-dominant peers, comparing installed capacity, renewable power per capita, hydropower potential, storage, grid structure, market design, and institutional context.

The Bhutan Model: Why It's Not Copyable

Bhutan Hydropower Model works because:

  1. Population: 0.8M vs Nepal's 30M — surplus per capita is massive
  2. Single buyer: India absorbs virtually all Bhutanese exports under guaranteed bilateral PPAs at fixed rates
  3. Fiscal structure: Hydro royalties + dividends fund ~25% of Bhutan's government budget
  4. Governance: Constitutional monarchy with policy continuity; Nepal has had 18+ governments in 30 years

Nepal cannot replicate the Bhutan model because it would need to export ~37x the volume to achieve equivalent per-capita revenue — far beyond any plausible corridor capacity — and its domestic demand and industrial base are large enough to be the primary consumption story rather than a rounding error.

The Laos Cautionary Tale

Laos has expanded aggressively (9,760 MW installed) through Chinese and Thai investment, targeting 13 GW by 2030. But:

  • Debt burden: Multiple dam projects are debt-financed by Chinese state banks; repayment dependent on Thai electricity purchases
  • Environmental cost: Mekong mainstream dams alter sediment and fish migration for 60+ million downstream people
  • Ownership: Much capacity is foreign-owned, with limited fiscal capture by the Laotian state
  • Nepal risks a similar "building for others" pattern if export-led strategy prioritizes MW over domestic productive use

Off-River Pumped Hydro Opportunity

A 2021 Oxford study identified ~2,800 off-river pumped hydro sites in Nepal with combined storage capacity of ~50 TWh. The same comparison uses ~1.5 TWh as the storage scale for balancing a 100% renewable system at advanced-economy consumption levels. That makes the cited off-river pumped-hydro potential roughly 17x the modeled balancing requirement — an option that bypasses the valley-shape paradox of on-river reservoir storage (see Storage Capacity Comparison).

Coverage / Method

The comparison assembles peer-country hydro, storage, and renewable-power-per-capita figures from the cited source pages and related benchmark material. Nepal's installed-capacity row is aligned with Hydropower Fleet Composition using the NEA FY 2024/25 hydro-only installed figure, while peer rows provide context for scale, storage, market structure, and institutional maturity.

Caveats

The rows combine countries with different reporting years, market designs, geologies, and ownership structures, so the table is a benchmark rather than a like-for-like project pipeline model. Potential figures, installed capacity, and per-capita indicators should be read as scale comparisons, not as direct evidence that one country's hydro model can be copied by another.
