Decision Dossier — Hetauda–Dhalkebar Completion and Community Resolution

An evidence-led assessment of options for completing and commissioning Hetauda–Dhalkebar while resolving community and right-of-way concerns; current completion and remaining-tower counts are not verified.

Important

TransparentGov assessment, not settled fact: Completion of the Hetauda–Dhalkebar section remains a high-value system objective, but the public evidence is insufficient to publish a current completion percentage, remaining-tower count, commissioning date or national curtailment benefit.

Decision Question

What combination of construction management, public disclosure, compensation and dispute-resolution measures can complete and commission the Hetauda–Dhalkebar section while addressing affected communities' right-of-way concerns?

Established Facts

  • NEA Annual Report FY 2024/25 reports that, at its July 2025 cutoff, 773 of 792 tower foundations were complete, 770 towers had been erected and more than 534 circuit-kilometres of conductor had been strung across the wider Hetauda–Dhalkebar–Inaruwa project. It also reports the Dhalkebar–Inaruwa section charged since June 2024.
  • NEA Hetauda–Dhalkebar Construction Notices, July 2026 records NEA local-outage notices on 3 and 5 July 2026 associated with conductor-stringing work on the Hetauda–Dhalkebar section. The notices establish active work, not completion or energisation.
  • World Bank NIETTP Hetauda–Dhalkebar–Inaruwa Resettlement Action Plan documents the corridor's right-of-way, land and structure impacts. These are material project-delivery issues, not merely a construction scheduling problem.
  • The public operational corpus does not contain the plant-level availability and dispatch data required to estimate national curtailed energy or attribute it to this corridor. See Stranded Generation and NEA Operational-Reporting Portals (2026).

Unknowns

  • Current erected-tower, foundation, stringing and testing totals after the July 2026 work.
  • Whether all required land, right-of-way and compensation agreements are complete.
  • A dated commissioning and energisation schedule issued by NEA.
  • Transfer capacity and N-1 improvement under current network conditions.
  • Avoided congestion, curtailment or export value attributable specifically to this section.
  • Terms of any current community agreement and whether affected households consider it implemented.

Comparable Case

Bharatpur–Bardaghat mediated settlement

The World Bank Accountability Mechanism record establishes that NEA and affected residents reached a mediated agreement in 2023 after the last two towers of the Bharatpur–Bardaghat 220 kV line had been delayed. The process used separate meetings, facilitated joint meetings, information exchange and negotiated options.

Transfer limitations: this was a different 220 kV line, alignment and community; it had access to a World Bank accountability mechanism; and the detailed settlement terms are confidential. It supports independent mediation as a demonstrated process option in Nepal. It does not show that Hetauda–Dhalkebar can use the same compensation terms, institution or timeline.

Response Options and Trade-offs

Option What it would involve Potential advantage Material limitation or risk
Publish and execute a dated completion plan NEA publishes remaining works, testing stages, responsible units and milestone updates Creates a common factual baseline and makes slippage visible Disclosure alone does not resolve disputed land, compensation or trust
Independent facilitated mediation A mutually accepted mediator structures information exchange and negotiation with affected communities Nepal has one documented transmission precedent Participation, authority and funding arrangements are not currently public; results cannot be guaranteed
Technical and social disclosure package Publish alignment impacts, compensation basis, EMF standards, grievance channels and implementation status in accessible language Addresses information asymmetry and permits claims to be checked May expose unresolved disagreement and require additional surveys or compensation review
Construction-led completion under existing authority Continue works wherever legal access and agreements are already in place May preserve momentum on uncontested spans Can deepen conflict if it is used in place of consultation, remedy or lawful compensation
Phased commissioning with published constraints Commission technically complete elements where system studies permit and disclose residual limits Could deliver partial operational value earlier Feasibility depends on engineering and protection studies not present in the public corpus

TransparentGov Assessment

Preferred evidence-led sequence: NEA should first publish a dated, segment-specific status and completion plan; then pair construction on uncontested works with an independent, documented process for unresolved community issues. The Bharatpur–Bardaghat case makes mediation a credible option, but not a proven template for the outcome.

This assessment would change if NEA publishes a commissioning certificate, an implemented settlement record, a revised route or a current works inventory. It does not assign a monetary benefit because the required load-flow and plant-level dispatch evidence is unavailable.

Monitoring Triggers

  • NEA energisation or commissioning notice for Hetauda–Dhalkebar.
  • Dated project inventory of foundations, towers, stringing, testing and substations.
  • Public compensation or grievance-resolution record.
  • Load-flow or transfer-capacity study.
  • Plant-level curtailment and dispatch dataset response.
