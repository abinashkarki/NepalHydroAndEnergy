Summary

The insurance market for Nepal's hydropower sector — coverage requirements, premium costs, exclusions and claim settlement. The current wiki record does not yet establish a sector-wide premium increase, settlement-delay rate or the contractual outcome of an insured outage under a PPA.

Simple Explanation

Hydropower projects need insurance because floods, landslides, GLOFs, and equipment failures can stop generation. The concept matters because insurance is not just a safety net: rising premiums, delayed claims, and lender requirements can become a major financial stress for IPPs.

Market Concentration

No market-concentration finding is currently made. It requires a complete insurer premium, exposure and reinsurance dataset with a defined period and methodology.

Claim Settlement Delays

The "90% claim settlement delays" claim is not verified. Upper Tamakoshi's FY 2080/81 statements record that property and lost-revenue claims were lodged after the September 2024 flood and remained in settlement processing at that report date; they do not give a later settlement outcome or a comparable market-wide delay measure. Interaction with AD Penalties and Force Majeure

The current secondary analysis of a standard PPA reports that force-majeure energy is included in the AD-penalty formula and that force majeure is narrowly defined. It does not establish whether a particular physical-damage loss qualifies, how an insurer's coverage decision affects it, or how NEA settled any resulting shortfall. Those questions require the signed PPA, policy wording, claim correspondence and settlement record for a named project. [Under research.]

Common Misunderstandings

  • Insurance does not remove project risk. It can shift some losses, but premium cost, exclusions, and settlement delays still affect cash flow.
  • A claim is not the same as cash in hand. Delayed settlement can matter as much as the covered amount.
  • Hydro insurance is not separate from PPA risk. Physical damage can interact with AD penalties, debt service, and off-taker receivables.
