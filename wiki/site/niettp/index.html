Summary

NIETTP is a World Bank-backed transmission and trade program linking Nepal-India exchange-capacity expansion with domestic 220/400 kV reinforcement works.

The World Bank-backed program that bundled Nepal's first large cross-border-capacity push with domestic reinforcement works. In the annual book's 2020 framing, NIETTP is the umbrella program linking the cross-border-exchange objective to both Hetauda–Dhalkebar–Inaruwa 400 kV Backbone and the Hetauda–Bharatpur–Bardaghat 220 kV Corridor reinforcement works.

Source note

nea-transmission-annual-book-2077 Book page 66 describes NIETTP as a World Bank-funded project intended to establish roughly 1,000 MW of Nepal–India high-voltage exchange capacity while also executing the Hetauda–Bharatpur–Bardaghat 220 kV line and associated substations.

Why It Matters

  • NIETTP is the programmatic bridge between domestic grid hardening and the export-opening logic that later runs through Dhalkebar–Muzaffarpur 400 kV Cross-Border Interconnection and India — Energy Relationship with Nepal.
  • It shows that Nepal's cross-border story was never just a border-wire story. The domestic substations and 220/400 kV reinforcements were part of the same reliability package.

Limitations & Controversies

This page summarizes NIETTP from NEA annual-book framing and does not independently reconcile each linked asset's latest completion status, financing closeout, commissioning history, or realized transfer capacity.
