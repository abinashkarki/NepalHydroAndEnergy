Summary

The wiki uses three public maturity labels to distinguish flagship evidence pages, useful work in progress and concise registry entries. Maturity is an editorial and evidence-readiness label, not a confidence score for every claim on a page.

Simple Explanation

Maturity applies to the page. Confidence applies to a claim. A Verified Core page can contain a low-confidence or unresolved item if it labels it honestly; a Registry Record can contain a high-confidence identity fact while remaining narratively thin.

Verified Core

A flagship page on the wiki's core reading path. It has an explicit excerpt, dated review, source provenance, a visible caveat and bounded claims. Verified Core does not mean complete or certain; important gaps must remain visible.

Working Page

A useful page with analysis or evidence that is still being reconciled, deepened or refreshed. It may contain strong individual claims, but the page as a whole has material gaps or does not yet meet the flagship review standard.

Registry Record

A concise structured record—often a project, organisation, place or dataset—whose basic identity or attributes are recorded. It may intentionally have little narrative. Registry Record must not be mistaken for a verified project status or complete dossier.

Common Misunderstandings

  • Verified Core does not mean complete or certain. Important gaps must remain visible.
  • Working Page does not mean every claim is weak. It may contain strong individual claims while the page as a whole still has material gaps.
  • Registry Record does not mean verified project status. It can be intentionally concise and narratively thin.
