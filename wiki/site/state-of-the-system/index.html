What changed

Nepal has moved from chronic scheduled load shedding to a larger, IPP-heavy generation fleet and annual net electricity exports. That transition is material: FY 2025/26 net exports of 2,794 GWh were four times the previous year's. The seasonal pattern did not disappear. In Falgun 2082 (February–March 2026), domestic generation was 801 GWh against national demand of 1,067 GWh; Nepal imported 273 GWh and exported 13 GWh. It does not mean that every hour or location has abundant, reliable electricity. Hydropower output remains seasonal; dry-season imports persist; transmission projects can lag generation; and a World Bank memorandum reports that 76% of surveyed firms experienced regular outages in 2022.

The World Bank records that more than 70% of generation capacity added since 2018 came from independent power producers, which held 64% of installed hydropower capacity by the end of 2024. Those figures describe ownership and fleet growth at that cutoff; they do not establish plant availability, dispatch priority or financial health. See World Bank Nepal Country Economic Memorandum (2025).

The physical system

Hydropower accounts for about 95% of installed capacity in the FY 2025/26 NEA snapshot. Because most hydro capacity is run-of-river or peaking run-of-river, river flow strongly shapes output. Seasonal Mismatch explains why wet-season surplus and dry-season weakness can coexist. Storage and System Flexibility covers the limited tools available to shift energy across seasons and hours.

Delivery, trade and end use

Electricity must move through internal transmission, distribution and cross-border interfaces before capacity becomes useful service or export revenue. The evidence shows material delivery constraints, but the public corpus does not contain plant-level dispatch and curtailment records needed to calculate a current national curtailment total. See Transmission and Cross-Border Electricity Trade and Distribution and Reliability.

Domestic customers account for most connections, while industry accounts for a much larger share of sales than its customer count. Grid reach is high, but use per person remains low and electric cooking remains limited. See Demand and Electrification.

Official direction is not current performance

Nepal's NDC 3.0 targets 14,031 MW of renewable electricity capacity by 2030 and 28,500 MW by 2035, alongside lower losses and expanded electric cooking. These are official policy targets, not verified delivery forecasts. Their implementation depends on finance, permits, transmission, demand, environmental and social safeguards, and project execution.

What is not yet measurable

  • A reconciled public plant-level series for availability, dispatch, outages and curtailment.
  • National and feeder-level SAIDI/SAIFI, voltage quality and restoration performance.
  • A current lifecycle-verified solar pipeline beyond operating-registry and award records.
  • Public hourly load, reserve, ancillary-service and storage state-of-charge data.
  • Fully reconciled progress evidence for several flagship projects and corridors.

These are maintained as evidence gaps in Unresolved Questions, not silently replaced with estimates.
