Summary

The planned third 400 kV Nepal–India link, critical to evacuating the Arun 3 export block (900 MW) and later Upper Karnali-tier eastern generation. Currently in implementation-setup phase — the route exists on paper and in the bilateral MoU record, but construction has not begun.

Key Facts

Parameter Value
Voltage 400 kV
Status Implementation setup
Nepal terminal Inaruwa substation (Sunsari) — eastern terminus of Hetauda–Dhalkebar–Inaruwa 400 kV Backbone
India terminal Purnea (Bihar)
Length ~160 km total (≈50 km Nepal, ≈110 km India)
Primary purpose Evacuation of Arun 3 to the Indian grid
Status driver Tied to Arun 3 COD — originally 2023, now 2027+
Interconnection ID inaruwa_purnea

Significance

  • Arun-3 depends on it. SJVN's 900 MW Arun 3 was approved under an export-first model. Without this line, its generation cannot reach the Indian market at rated capacity — a direct instance of Stranded Generation.
  • Third 400 kV cross-border. Combined with Dhalkebar–Muzaffarpur 400 kV Cross-Border Interconnection and Gorakhpur–New Butwal 400 kV Cross-Border Interconnection, it would give Nepal three east/central/west 400 kV export paths — the first real geographic redundancy.
  • Koshi basin anchor. Establishes the first 400 kV link serving the Koshi Basin directly, opening the door to future eastern storage (e.g. Dudhkoshi Storage Hydroelectric Project).

Limitations & Controversies

  • Schedule tied to Arun-3. Each slip in Arun-3 construction pushes the cross-border line as well — and vice versa.
  • Environmental route. Alignment through Sunsari and Saptari districts still contested in local consultations.
  • Indian-side approvals. Bihar state government concurrence and CEA tariff framework are the remaining gating items.
