Summary

Economic Consulting Associates Limited prepared this April 2025 discussion paper for Nepal's Electricity Regulatory Commission. Its stated purpose is to develop proposed pricing arrangements for reservoir-hydropower PPAs between the Nepal Electricity Authority and independent power producers, subject to ERC approval. The cover states that ERC could consider the findings fully or partly after stakeholder input; the paper is not itself a directive or an approved project tariff.

Key Findings

  • The sector overview describes Nepal's hydro capacity as predominantly run-of-river, with a smaller set of peaking run-of-river plants providing a few hours of storage, and identifies Kulekhani I as the existing reservoir plant. The statement is a sector summary tied to the paper's 2023/24 data boundary.
  • The paper records standard NEA tariffs issued in April 2017 for RoR, PRoR, and reservoir projects in NPR per kilowatt-hour. It says ERC understood the reservoir tariffs to have been intended for Kulekhani I and subsequently withdrawn.
  • The introduction treats attracting reservoir investment at acceptable cost as the objective and identifies large-hydro risk, Nepal's political and macroeconomic uncertainty, reliance on international capital, and investment de-risking as tariff-design considerations.
  • The paper compares value-based and cost-based pricing, estimated and actual construction costs, debt-service timing, foreign-exchange exposure, and allocation of construction and volume risk.
  • Its charging-structure section considers separating fixed or capacity-based recovery from variable or energy-based recovery. It discusses available capacity as a possible basis for the fixed component and generated energy as the basis for the variable component.
  • Annex A1 contains named tables of existing and planned plants with RoR, PRoR, reservoir, and pumped-storage classifications. These are useful plant records within the annex's stated date and source boundary.

Relevance

The paper is cited by PPA Pricing for the difference between energy-priced standard tariffs and the capacity-and-energy questions raised for reservoir procurement. It is also cited by Operating Fleet Is Overwhelmingly RoR/PRoR, Not True Storage as one current official-sector description of the operating mix.

Limitations

  • This is a consultant discussion paper prepared for ERC, not the final 2082 directive and not a signed PPA.
  • The paper proposes a pricing approach; it does not establish that ERC adopted every proposal.
  • Its sector overview and annex use the capacity and project-status records available for the 2023/24 context. They are not a current operating register.
  • The paper is not a retrospective causal study of why Nepal's fleet became RoR/PRoR-heavy. It supports fleet description, existing tariff form, and differentiated reservoir risk/finance—not a single-cause account.
  • Illustrative tariff calculations use hypothetical project parameters and are explicitly not regulatory precedent.
