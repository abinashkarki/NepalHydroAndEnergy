Damak–Godak–Phidim 132 kV Corridor

The eastern 132 kV evacuation chain that the annual book describes in three stages: Damak–Godak, Godak–Phidim, and onward Phidim–Amarpur (Kabeli). It is the practical corridor through which the Mai/Kabeli-side hill projects were being tied back into the national system.

The eastern 132 kV evacuation chain that the annual book describes in three stages: Damak–Godak, Godak–Phidim, and onward Phidim–Amarpur (Kabeli). It is the practical corridor through which the Mai/Kabeli-side hill projects were being tied back into the national system.

Summary

The Damak-Godak-Phidim 132 kV corridor is an eastern Nepal evacuation chain documented in the NEA 2077 annual book. The page tracks the Damak-Godak, Godak-Phidim, and Phidim-Amarpur/Kabeli sections as a corridor-scale grid asset for Mai/Kabeli-side projects.

Source note

nea-transmission-annual-book-2077 Book page 45 states that the first Damak–Godak section was already in service, the Godak–Phidim section was completed and charged in 2019, and the Phidim–Amarpur (Kabeli) section was commissioned on 6 August 2019.

Key Facts

Segment Length / note
Damak–Godak ~35 km
Godak–Phidim ~43 km
Phidim–Amarpur (Kabeli) ~13.33 km

Why It Matters

  • This is the corridor-scale version of the eastern medium-hill evacuation problem: relatively modest IPP clusters still needed a sequence of new substations and 132 kV links before their output could move.
  • It is directly relevant to project pages such as Kabeli-A, Kabeli B - 1, and the broader Transmission and Delivery Are Material Immediate Monetization Constraints argument.

Limitations & Controversies

The page relies on the annual-book corridor description and does not provide a full route-grade GIS alignment, tower list, updated asset condition, or post-commissioning loading record. Segment lengths should be read as source-reported corridor context rather than independently surveyed geometry.
