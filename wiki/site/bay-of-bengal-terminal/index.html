Summary

This marker is the terminal reach of the generated route-to-ocean trace.

Simple Explanation

It is the map point that says: the Nepal-origin downstream route does not stop at the Indian plains. It continues through the lower delta system until it reaches the Bay of Bengal.

Why It Matters

It gives the map a complete endpoint. Without it, the downstream river story visually stops in the plains or delta, even though the strategic route continues through the lower Padma-Meghna system to the sea.

How To Read The Marker

Use it as the end of the strategic route trace, not as a detailed coastal or tidal model.

Caveat

The terminal point comes from the HydroRIVERS route trace. It does not represent all distributaries, estuarine channels, or coastal sediment dynamics.

Common Misunderstandings

  • It is not a coastal model. The point marks the route endpoint, not tides, ports, erosion, or delta morphology.
  • It is not a claim that Nepal controls downstream outcomes. It only completes the visual trace from Himalayan basins to the sea.
  • It is not a precise distributary selection. The lower delta contains many channels; this marker is a strategic route endpoint.
