Summary

In Section 2.4 of MoEWRI IPSDP Executive Summary, the IPSDP does two different but related things. It names three generator-facing wholesale customer categories, and it separately describes the commercial modes those channels may use, including exchange-market sales and long-term PPAs.

Simple Explanation

Nepal power trade is not one single export mechanism. The IPSDP describes a mixed structure where power can move through NEA, trading companies, or direct overseas buyers, and those channels can use either long-term PPAs or more market-linked sales.

Generator-facing wholesale channels named in the IPSDP

Channel Buyer How the PDF frames it
NEA default Nepal Electricity Authority (NEA) Conventional domestic trade. NEA buys power, serves the domestic market, and exports surplus itself. Lowest administrative burden for smaller projects.
Power-trading company Nepal trading company such as NPTCL A future channel in which trading companies intermediate between generators and markets. The PDF explicitly says sales may be either PPA-based or market-linked.
Direct overseas trade DISCOs or large customers in India/Bangladesh A generator deals directly with overseas buyers. The PDF ties this to larger projects, investor participation, and more complex transmission and contracting requirements.

Commercial modes layered on top of those channels

The executive summary then adds a second distinction that should not be collapsed into the buyer categories above:

  • Long-term PPA mode: PPAs with NEA, with a trading company, or directly with foreign distribution companies / major customers.
  • Market-linked mode: power sold through trading-company arrangements into exchange-style markets such as IEX, including the green-market discussion in the surrounding text.

That is why the section reads as more than a simple "three-scheme" menu. The PDF clearly names three buyer categories, but it also makes room for multiple price-setting and dispatch arrangements inside them.

Why this matters to the wiki

  • It clarifies that Nepal is not moving from one monolithic single-buyer system to one monolithic export system. The IPSDP is imagining a mixed commercial stack.
  • It sharpens India — Energy Relationship with Nepal and Nepal–Bangladesh Electricity Trade Route by showing that cross-border trade is not just a transmission problem; it is also a buyer-structure and contract-structure problem.
  • It explains why Nepal–India Electricity Transmission and Trade Project (NIETTP) and other interconnection corridors are enabling infrastructure rather than the whole trade model by themselves.

Confidence boundary

This page sticks to what the executive summary says explicitly on pages 24-26. It does not claim that Nepal has already implemented this market structure. The document is a planning frame, not proof of execution.

Common Misunderstandings

  • Trade schemes are not the same as transmission corridors. Lines enable trade, but buyer structure and contract mode determine how trade works.
  • Direct overseas trade is not already normal practice. In this page it is an IPSDP planning category, not proof of implementation.
  • Market-linked sales and long-term PPAs are different commercial modes. They should not be collapsed into one generic export bucket.
