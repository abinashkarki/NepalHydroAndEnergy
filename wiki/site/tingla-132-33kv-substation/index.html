Summary

Tingla 132/33 kV Substation is a commissioned 30 MVA Solu Corridor node that connects Solu basin generation into the 132 kV system.

A 132/33 kV, 30 MVA substation commissioned as part of the Solu corridor build. In the annual-book snapshot, Tingla is the bridge intended to connect Solu basin IPP output into the larger 132 kV system once the Mirchaiya–Tingla line is complete.

Source note

nea-transmission-annual-book-2077 Book page 45 states that Tingla substation (132/33 kV, 30 MVA) was commissioned on Bhadra 07, 2076, and identifies it as part of the Solu Corridor 132 kV transmission line project.

Key Facts

Parameter Value

Corridor Solu Corridor 132 kV
Upstream line noted Mirchaiya–Tingla 132 kV double circuit, ~90 km

Why It Matters

  • Tingla is a concrete example of the smaller corridor substations that had to exist before generation in eastern hill basins could actually reach the grid.
  • It belongs in the same “transmission before value” story as Transmission and Delivery Are Material Immediate Monetization Constraints and the later Nepal Transmission System — 400 kV Landscape & Cross-Border Plan (2025) synthesis.

Limitations & Controversies

The page confirms the annual-book commissioning record and basic rating, but it does not establish current transformer loading, bay configuration, outage history, or subsequent upgrades after the 2020 source snapshot.
