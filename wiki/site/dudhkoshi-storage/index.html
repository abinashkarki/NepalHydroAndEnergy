Dudhkoshi Storage Hydroelectric Project

A proposed 670 MW storage scheme with a current design and appraisal trail, but no verified financial close or construction start.

Caveat

Proposed multilateral financing and market sounding do not establish approval, procurement completion or construction readiness.

Summary

A proposed 670 MW storage reservoir scheme straddling Okhaldhunga, Khotang and Solukhumbu in the eastern Koshi Basin. The February 2026 AIIB and DKJVCL materials describe a 600 MW underground main powerhouse and a 70 MW dam-toe ecological-flow powerhouse. The project is in pre-construction development and market sounding: AIIB financing is proposed, not approved or effective, and no current primary source establishes a firm construction start or commercial-operation date.

Engineering

Dudhkoshi's current published design pairs a 220 m dam with an underground main powerhouse (600 MW) and a dam-toe ecological-flow powerhouse (70 MW). The current AIIB project summary and 2026 market-sounding teaser do not establish the previously listed 200 MW pumped-storage component, so that figure has been removed from the structured specification record pending a primary design source. The project's safeguard work explicitly includes sediment, slope, seismic, biodiversity and GLOF Risk assessment.

That figure remains in the structured registry, but the current AIIB summary does not publish every reservoir parameter; detailed design documents should remain the authority for gross, live and operating storage. Compare the scale with Kulekhani Cascade and the 140 MW Tanahu Hydropower Project project under construction.

Significance

Dudhkoshi matters on three documented axes:

  1. Scale. 670 MW of proposed reservoir generation in a system where the entire current storage fleet is 106 MW (Kulekhani Cascade) and the only storage actually under construction is 140 MW (Tanahu Hydropower Project). Dudhkoshi alone would roughly match the combined 2025 hydropower fleet of Nepal on energy volume and materially change the dry-season Firm Power portfolio if built.

  2. Seasonal storage. The reservoir and 1,252 GWh dry-season design figure make the project relevant to Seasonal Mismatch without requiring the unsupported pumped-storage claim.

  3. Safeguard-intensive design. The project is being scoped with landscape stabilization and sediment management built in — a response to Nepal's Sediment as Design Constraint reality and to the fact that the upper Dudh Koshi routinely receives GLOF pulses from the Everest region (see GLOF Risk).

Compare Dudhkoshi (670 MW, storage, proposed multilateral financing) to Arun 3 (900 MW, peaking RoR, SJVN, export-first). Both are in the Koshi basin, but they occupy different delivery stages and have different operating purposes.

Limitations & Controversies

Four-decade gestation. The original feasibility was ADB-supported in the 1980s. Land acquisition restarted only in 2021. A project that has been "about to be built" for 40 years carries a credibility tax that any new timeline must earn against.

Displacement footprint. AIIB's preliminary summary reports about 3,300 affected households and approximately 290 households expected to be physically and economically displaced. The transmission line and access-road impacts were still being assessed at publication, so these figures are not a final project-wide resettlement total.

GLOF exposure. The 2024 Thame Valley GLOF traveled down the Dudh Koshi mainstem. A 220 m dam on a GLOF-exposed river demands a design flood that correctly accounts for cascading lake outburst scenarios, not a stationary climatology. How that design flood is set — and how conservative the spillway margin is — is the safety-critical question for the entire project.

Financing status. AIIB sovereign financing is proposed, not approved, effective or disbursed. The disclosed USD 200 million amount also covers only part of the estimated project cost.
